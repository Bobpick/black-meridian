          Evaluation guidance

          What good looks like

          • Commanders identify what they know, what they do not know, and what they will do about the gap.
          • Orders communicate purpose clearly enough for subordinate initiative during C2 disruption.
          • Sustainment and recovery are treated as operational choices, not administrative afterthoughts.
          • Reserves preserve future options instead of merely filling the nearest gap.
          • Civilian and political consequences are included in military risk decisions.
          • Plans change when evidence changes, and the reason for the change is recorded.

          Why it matters for households

          Mobilization, training, existing missions, legal authority, transportation, and local conditions determine when a capability affects a community. National totals should therefore be used as context—not as a local safety score.

          Reality checks

          What public force numbers cannot tell you

          These constraints keep the simulation from producing false certainty.

          Model hygiene
          • End strength is an annual authorization and personnel target, not a count of ready units.
          • Readiness is uneven across formations and changes with training, maintenance, staffing, and tasking.
          • Domestic response has legal, political, state, and local constraints.
          • Family outcomes are usually mediated by power, water, fuel, communications, health care, payments, transportation, and supply chains.

          What this tool is—and is not

          Read first
          • A structured way to explore uncertainty and household continuity.
          • A place to make assumptions visible and revise them.
          • A scenario-planning aid that uses public aggregate information.
          • Not an intelligence product, threat warning, evacuation order, or personal safety guarantee.
          • Not a substitute for official emergency alerts or local authorities.
