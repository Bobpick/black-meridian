Family first.
Small groups. Hard wall.

This is for a kitchen table, a church basement, or four or five neighbor households. Every household that does the work is an asset to its own line of people. This file is an asset to that household. Named countries, invented events. It does not predict war, write a war plan, or hunt anyone. It helps you keep people you love able to live through a shock.

0 / Home front

Run this first if the goal is family

BLACK MERIDIAN is free. Use the Home-front session for 90 minutes with a small group. You are not a combatant command. You are parents, grandparents, and neighbors. Pick a hotspot radio: Taiwan Strait (most discussed), Canada / northern trade, Mexico / southern trade, or Homeland worst night. The nightmare radio is effects at home — jammed lines, ugly phones, late medicine, rumor. Canada and Mexico are congestion and story, not invasion routes. Do not name armies by race or religion. The test is whether these households still have water, medicine, a check-in, and a rule: we do not hunt people.

Effects, not targeting: describe power, water, fuel, communications, medical access, and payments failing. Do not name real plants, bases, bridges, or hospitals as targets. Do not store real names or addresses.
  1. 0–10 min: ethics and region.
  2. 10–25 min: household baselines and callsign roster.
  3. 25–70 min: injects H-01, H-03, H-04, H-05. After each: stay, move, share, or wait.
  4. 70–90 min: three 14-day commitments, each with a test. Export the session log.

The score is closed gaps on a re-run — people who can still be a family — not a campaign victory.

Hard wall (Kobayashi Maru): the war is unwinnable from this table. You pass when the group can say: we cannot win the war, and we can still be a family on the worst night — water, medicine, check-in, no hunting.

Radio mile markers: after each inject, receive a dispatch on W-BM Family Net. First you hear what happened in the world. Then you hear whether this table’s plan held, was partial, or failed — scored from the household baseline, not from hope. Untested comms copy late.

1 / Scope

What this tool is and is not

Named countries, invented campaign: The United States, China, Taiwan, Japan, and the Philippines appear as public-scenario actors. Units, injects, and the map are notional. This is not an operational plan or targeting aid.
It is

A structured decision exercise

A way to examine uncertainty, command friction, sustainment, information quality, political constraints, and household continuity.

It is not

An intelligence product

It does not establish that a threat is imminent, forecast an attack, identify targets, or calculate whether a particular household will survive.

The most useful output is not a single prediction. It is a set of plans that remain useful across several different futures.

3 / Quick start

Recommended first session

01

Read the boundary

Start with the Home-front session and this scope section. A named Taiwan Strait crisis is a planning assumption, not evidence that war is coming.

02

Open the U.S. reality layer

Review the public aggregate force figures and their limitations. Do not treat authorized end strength as ready, local, or immediately available personnel.

03

Select a broad region

Use Regional planner to choose the closest planning archetype. If your household crosses regions, create a profile for each relevant location.

04

Complete the household baseline

Record only simple counts and status choices: household size, water and food days, communications, medical continuity, transport, dependents, and documents.

05

Review the gap list

Close high-consequence gaps first. The result is a planning-completeness view—not a probability score or safety guarantee.

06

Run one short tabletop

Pick one horizon—72 hours, 14 days, or several months. Ask what changes if power, roads, communications, or medical access are disrupted. Record assumptions in Orders & logs.

07

Score and export

Use Evaluation for the campaign exercise, then export the profile, orders, scorecard, or scenario JSON into storage/.

4 / Public data

Using the U.S. reality layer

The dashboard uses public aggregate data to give the fictional exercise a scale reference. The active and reserve figures are authorization/end-strength figures. They help describe national personnel scale, but they do not answer questions about local readiness, deployment, warning time, or civilian effects.

Interpret the numbers in four steps

  1. Scale: use them as a national reference, not a local headcount.
  2. Availability: ask what missions, training cycles, maintenance, legal authorities, and transportation affect availability.
  3. Geography: identify which services and infrastructure connect your region to the rest of the country.
  4. Civilian effects: model power, water, fuel, communications, medical care, payments, transportation, and supply chains.

The dashboard currently cites Congressional Research Service FY2026 active and reserve end-strength reports, FEMA’s National Risk Index, Ready.gov, and CISA communications-resilience guidance. Recheck source dates before using the figures for a new exercise.

5 / Regional planner

How to use regional modules

Choose the region that best represents the household’s location. These are broad planning archetypes, not forecasts. A county, tribal nation, city, or utility service area can differ substantially from the regional label.

Validate

Use official local sources

Check FEMA’s county/tract data, state and county emergency-management plans, local alert systems, utility outage information, road conditions, and relevant NOAA or USGS products.

Compare

Use two locations when needed

If family members live in different regions, create separate profiles and identify the communication and travel assumptions that connect them.

Each region provides

  • Primary and secondary hazard categories
  • Critical infrastructure and access dependencies
  • First-72-hour planning prompts
  • Days-4-to-14 continuity prompts
  • Weeks-to-months recovery prompts
  • A validation checklist for public local data

The form is intentionally simple. It is designed to identify missing preparations, not to rank people or locations.

7 / Campaign exercise

How to run BLACK MERIDIAN

Exercise-control setup

  • Players: tactical commanders or staffs.
  • Red cell: opposing-force decisions based on its own objectives and incomplete information.
  • Gray/civil cell: neutral authorities, civilians, humanitarian actors, media, and coalition politics.
  • Exercise Control: hidden-state tracking, inject release, adjudication, and evaluation.

Turn procedure

  1. State the current time and known situation.
  2. Players issue intent, task, purpose, assumptions, risk, sustainment priority, and decision points.
  3. Red, civil, and higher-headquarters cells respond.
  4. Exercise Control resolves effects using the player picture, the actual situation, friction, and sustainment state.
  5. Return tactical, operational, civil, political, and information effects.
  6. Record one lesson before advancing the clock.

Suggested time rhythm

SituationSimulated resolutionUse
Routine campaign12–24 hoursPlanning, movement, sustainment, recovery.
High-friction period4–6 hoursConflicting reports, degraded C2, civilian pressure.
Critical decisionDecision pointReserve use, mission change, ceasefire, escalation.

Do not score only attrition

Track decision quality, information confidence, command resilience, sustainment, reserve management, civilian effects, coalition cohesion, and whether the force culminates before achieving its objectives.

8 / Outputs

Evaluation, exports, and storage

  • Orders & logs: save tactical decision records in browser-local storage.
  • Evaluation: score six measures from 1 to 5 and add after-action notes.
  • Regional planner: export a regional brief and household profile.
  • Export scenario: save the baseline fictional scenario as JSON.

Move downloaded files into black-meridian/storage/. The browser cannot reliably save directly into an arbitrary folder when an HTML file is opened locally.

9 / Maintenance

Keeping the model current

A

Review official sources

Check FEMA, Ready.gov, local emergency management, NOAA/National Weather Service, USGS, utilities, state DOTs, and health agencies for changes.

B

Separate facts from assumptions

Keep dated source data separate from scenario assumptions. Mark uncertain values as ranges or confidence bands.

C

Update the source JSON

Edit the appropriate file in data/. Because the dashboard is self-contained, update its embedded data block when you want the offline HTML to reflect the change.

D

Retest the dashboard

Open the HTML, switch through every tab, test an export, and verify that the exported file opens as valid JSON.

10 / Interpretation

Safety and decision discipline

  • Use official alerts and instructions for real events. This dashboard does not replace emergency management or first-responder guidance.
  • Do not treat a scenario inject, public force total, or regional archetype as evidence that an event is about to occur.
  • Do not publish personal addresses, medical identifiers, account information, or sensitive family details in exported files.
  • For immediate danger, contact local emergency services and follow official evacuation or shelter instructions.
  • Review the family plan periodically, calmly, and with the people who would actually use it.
Best practice: build plans that are useful for ordinary disruptions—power loss, severe weather, transport failure, medical interruption, or wildfire smoke. Those preparations remain valuable even when the extreme scenario never happens.
